## Introduction

You might be pondering about GitLab, contemplating its benefits, or querying its relevance to your needs. The only person who can truly answer whether GitLab is a fit for you is, indeed, you. This guide aims to provide a detailed overview of GitLab, its features, pricing, and how you can start using it, making your decision process easier.

## Why GitLab?

GitLab, a modern and continuously improving tool, has been rapidly gaining market share as a leading platform for continuous integration and delivery. More traditional tools like Jenkins, which have not evolved significantly, are losing ground to GitLab and similar solutions.

The reasons for this shift are many. GitLab's modern architecture, which is Docker-oriented and easily scalable, is one. This architecture supports pipeline-as-code, making tasks more efficient. GitLab also offers a range of features like built-in repositories, merge requests, and issue tracking.

Here's an example: When you're working on a project, you can create a 'merge request' that encapsulates your changes. You can then track any issues that arise during the development process, all within GitLab itself.

## Exploring GitLab Features

To determine whether GitLab suits your needs, the best approach is hands-on experience. This guide will introduce you to the important features of GitLab, particularly focusing on GitLab CI for continuous integration and deployment.

Though the use of the repository part of GitLab is quite similar to other solutions, GitLab shines with its integrated continuous integration solution.

## Understanding GitLab Pricing

You might be wondering about the cost of GitLab. There are two primary ways you can run GitLab - on GitLab.com or on your own server.

1. **GitLab.com**: This option offers a free package that provides 2000 pipeline minutes. This refers to the execution time of your script when running your pipeline. For the scope of this guide, 2000 minutes (over 30 hours) should be sufficient. However, if you require more features or pipeline minutes, paid options are available per user per month.

2. **Self-hosted**: This option allows you to host GitLab on your own server. Here, you can use the powerful and free Community Edition of GitLab, though it comes without support.

Your choice depends on your specific needs. The cloud offering from GitLab doesn't require any installation on your premises, whereas the self-hosted option provides more control but also requires you to handle installation, updates, backups, etc.

## Starting with GitLab

For beginners, GitLab.com is likely the most accessible option. The good news is, you can migrate to a self-hosted solution later if you choose. The migration process involves cloning repositories, adjusting settings, and transferring the jobs created on GitLab.com to your own server.

## Conclusion

GitLab is a versatile, modern tool for continuous integration and delivery. Whether it's the right choice for you depends on your specific needs, but its features and scalability make it a strong contender in the market. Get hands-on experience to see if it's a good fit for you.
